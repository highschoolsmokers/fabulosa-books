A Cat Calendar on the Wall

The store

Fabulosa Books is an independent bookstore in San Francisco's Castro. It specializes in LGBTQIA+ titles, with those sections displayed prominently at the front of the shop. I work there, and I built this scheduler for the store. This tutorial walks through the same build, one file at a time.

The staff are the real crew, by first name. Their availability in the examples is made up; the store's hours and its scheduling problem are real.

The store's history

The Castro is named for José Castro, a governor of Alta California. The neighborhood took shape in 1887, when a streetcar line linked Eureka Valley to downtown.

Through the 1960s and 1970s the district changed from a working-class neighborhood into one with some of the highest concentrations of same-sex couples. Young gay people, mostly men, came from across the country to a place where their sexual orientation was accepted.

Walk a block south from the store, and you reach 575 Castro Street. Harvey Milk ran Castro Camera there from 1972 until he was assassinated in 1978.

Milk was the first openly gay man elected to public office in California. He served less than a year on the Board of Supervisors before he was killed, along with Mayor George Moscone, in November 1978.

The shop became the headquarters for Milk's campaigns, and people called him the "Mayor of Castro Street." A metal plaque in the sidewalk out front memorializes him.

In 1978, while Milk was in office, 489 Castro Street was the Obelisk, a designer gift shop.

I see the building's obelisk-shaped door handles every day.

A Different Light, a chain of LGBT bookstores that began in Los Angeles in 1979, opened its San Francisco branch at 489 Castro Street later in the 1980s. In January 1989, Richard Labonté became its manager.

The chain's Lesbian Writers Series had begun in 1984, before the Castro branch opened. Its one rule was that every reader's name went into the publicity. Playwright James Carroll Pickett launched the Gay Writers Series and hosted its monthly readings from 1987 to 1990.

The store opened its space to outside groups, among them ACT UP (the AIDS Coalition To Unleash Power), HomoCore, and GLOE (Gay & Lesbian Outreach and Engagement).

HomoCore was a San Francisco zine that Tom Jennings and Deke Nihilson published from 1988 to 1991 for young hardcore punks in the gay underground.

The chain closed in 2011. Its San Francisco branch was the last one still open.

Dog Eared Books moved into the space in 2016. In 2021 the manager bought the business and reopened it as Fabulosa Books on September 15.

Three bookstores have occupied the building. Fabulosa Books still sells queer books there, and I work there.

A cat calendar on the wall

The schedule hung behind the register on a cat calendar. The manager wrote it in by hand each week.

To see a shift, you came into the store and read the wall. To trade one, you crossed out a name and put another in its place.

Writing the names in was the easy part. The manager also carried everything the calendar could not show: who couldn't work Sundays, who had asked for a Wednesday off, who held keys, who was close to the weekly limit, and who had closed three Saturdays running.

What you'll build

A web app where Alvin types "Schedule next week" and gets back a complete, legal week to review. Behind the chat is one scheduling agent with seven tools. It reads the staff and their availability, proposes a week, reads back every rule the week breaks, and fixes the week until it breaks none. A fairness reviewer checks how the closings and weekend shifts fall. Nothing saves until Alvin approves it in the chat.

The stack:

  • Next.js 16 for the app and its one API route.
  • AI SDK 7 for the agent loop, the tools, and the chat UI.
  • Vercel AI Gateway for model access, with no provider key in the code.
  • Neon for Postgres.
  • Vitest for the tests, including tests that drive the agent with a scripted model.

Every code block in this tutorial comes from the finished app. A test in the app fails if a block here stops matching the source.

What you need

  • Node.js 22 or later.
  • A Vercel account, for the AI Gateway and the Neon database.
  • Some TypeScript and React. The tutorial explains the agent code line by line and moves faster through the rest.
